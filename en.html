Privacy Policy

METLA Cleaner · Last updated: November 20, 2026

This policy explains what information the METLA Cleaner app for Android (“METLA”) reads and sends: why, who keeps it and for how long. It describes METLA versions newer than 1.3.0.

  • Your files, your list of apps, usage statistics and other apps’ permissions never leave your device. METLA does not share your list of apps with anyone — not with us and not with other companies. The only exception is the error text in a crash report, if you agreed to crash reports (section 3).
  • Little leaves the device: an anonymous account and service data that plans, server protection and METLA’s switches in case of a failure cannot work without. Analytics and crash reports are sent only after you agree. All of it is listed in section 3.
  • We do not sell information about you. METLA currently shows no ads and does not collect the advertising ID.

2. What stays on your device

METLA reads and processes the following only on your device. The app has no way to send any of it over the network. The only exception is the error text in a crash report (section 3).

  • Files. To find junk, duplicates, thumbnails, empty folders, old and large files and to move what you delete to Quarantine, METLA needs file access: on Android 11 and above — All files access; on Android 8–10 — the Storage permission and the folders you choose. The files themselves and the scan results never leave the device. METLA does not read other apps’ private folders (Android/data and Android/obb).
  • List of installed apps: app names, icons, sizes and permissions — for the Apps and Activity screens, Anti-Spy and Radar.
  • Usage statistics — a special access you grant yourself in Android settings: how often and how long apps run and when they ran in the background — for Apps, Activity, Radar and tips; the size of other apps’ cache — for Storage, Messengers and the daily tip.
  • Other apps’ permissions (Anti-Spy): which of seven kinds of data — camera, microphone, location, contacts, phone, SMS, storage — an app has access to and which it only requests.
  • The QR scanner for Family invites is a Google Play service on the device: it controls the camera, and METLA receives only the scanned code. METLA has no camera permission. The service itself may send Google information about how it works — under Google’s rules.
  • Profile: your “About me” text and profile photo.
  • Cleaning history, Quarantine and METLA settings.

METLA reads the list of apps, usage statistics and other apps’ permissions only after you tap “Agree and continue” on the Before You Continue screen. If you chose “Not now”, those screens show a placeholder, and you can agree later. Two things work without this consent: Messengers checks whether the five messengers on its list (WhatsApp, WhatsApp Business, Telegram, TikTok, Instagram) are installed, to show their files; and METLA shows the size of other apps’ cache if you yourself granted it usage access in Android settings — a total for the whole device plus the messengers’ sizes. None of this leaves the device. Exception: if Radar was turned on in METLA 1.3 or earlier and you chose “Not now” on the Before You Continue screen, Radar keeps checking the apps on its list until you turn it off.

3. What leaves your device

METLA runs on Google cloud services. Below is everything that leaves your device: when, why, who keeps it and for how long. Google Firebase and Google Cloud process this information for us as a service provider; Google Play, the backup in your Google account and Telegram are independent services that work under their own rules. Everything is sent over a secure connection (HTTPS). We do not sell this information and do not use it for advertising.

4. Permissions

There are no permission prompts at launch: METLA asks for each permission when you tap a button where it is needed. If you decline twice, METLA offers to open Android settings. Below is the full list of METLA’s permissions, including the service permissions added by Google and Android libraries.

METLA’s permissions

  • All files access (MANAGE_EXTERNAL_STORAGE), Android 11 and above: finding junk, duplicates and empty folders, moving what you delete to Quarantine and restoring files. Without it METLA cannot see photos, videos, music and documents and cannot delete files. METLA has no separate photo, video or music permissions.
  • Storage (READ_EXTERNAL_STORAGE), Android 8–10: the same tasks in the folders you chose; METLA deletes only inside them.
  • List of installed apps: Apps, Activity, Anti-Spy and Radar (section 2). METLA 1.5.1 and later need no separate permission for this: on Android 11 and later METLA sees apps that have a launcher icon and the five messengers on its list (a <queries> declaration); on Android 8–10 it sees all installed apps, because Android does not limit this there. In METLA 1.5.0 the list came through the QUERY_ALL_PACKAGES permission.
  • Usage statistics (PACKAGE_USAGE_STATS) — a special access in Android settings (section 2).
  • Uninstalling apps (REQUEST_DELETE_PACKAGES): uninstall an app you picked — you confirm it in the Android dialog.
  • Stopping background work (KILL_BACKGROUND_PROCESSES): ask Android to stop an app’s background work; works on Android 8–13.
  • Notifications (POST_NOTIFICATIONS), Android 13 and above: auto-clean results, junk and trial reminders, Radar alerts. You can turn them off in METLA Settings.
  • Network state (ACCESS_WIFI_STATE, ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE): Device Pulse shows whether Wi-Fi is on and whether there is a network; libraries check for a connection.
  • Bluetooth (BLUETOOTH), Android 8–11: Device Pulse shows whether Bluetooth is on.

Library service permissions

  • Internet (INTERNET): our server, Firebase, Google Play and the pages of these documents.
  • Purchases (BILLING): payments through Google Play.
  • Task scheduler (WAKE_LOCK, RECEIVE_BOOT_COMPLETED, FOREGROUND_SERVICE): scheduled auto-clean, Radar and reminders, including after a restart.
  • Google push notification service (RECEIVE): the device ID that Firebase libraries attach to requests (section 3, “App integrity check”).
  • Install source (BIND_GET_INSTALL_REFERRER_SERVICE): analytics learns where the app was installed from — only after you agree.
  • Google service permission (READ_GSERVICES): added by the protection of the Firebase sign-in service (reCAPTCHA).
  • Biometrics (USE_BIOMETRIC, USE_FINGERPRINT): added by an Android library; METLA does not use biometrics.
  • Internal permission (DYNAMIC_RECEIVER_NOT_EXPORTED_PERMISSION): a service permission of the AndroidX library that works only inside METLA.

What METLA does not have: the advertising ID and the related Android ad services permissions, camera permission, photo, video and music permissions, write-to-storage, Bluetooth device connection and location permissions.

5. Quarantine, history and backup

Files you delete in METLA go to Quarantine first. On Android 11 and above a file is moved without copying into the hidden .METLA_Trash folder at the root of the same storage (device storage or memory card); on Android 8–10 Quarantine keeps a copy in METLA’s internal storage. Quarantine exists only on your device. Space is freed when a file leaves Quarantine.

On Android 11 and above the Quarantine folder is in the device’s shared storage: file managers and other apps with All files access can see it.

The default retention period is 30 days; for free you can choose 7, 14 or 30 days, and with Pro also 60 days or “No time limit”. “Auto-clean Quarantine” is on by default: when the period ends, the file is deleted permanently. The default Quarantine size is 1 GB (from 256 MB to 10 GB). When it runs out of space, METLA warns you and asks before permanently deleting the oldest files.

A file can be restored while it is in Quarantine and METLA has file access (on Android 11 and above — All files access). On Android 8–9 a file can be restored only to the folders you chose.

If you uninstall METLA on Android 11 and above, the .METLA_Trash folder remains — you can delete it with any file manager; after reinstalling, METLA does not see the earlier files in it. On Android 8–10 Quarantine is erased together with METLA, and also by “Clear storage” in Android settings.

Cleaning history is stored only on the device; you can clear it in Profile. METLA does not make an Android backup at all — neither to the cloud nor when moving to a new device: history, Quarantine, settings and the profile photo do not move over. Your previous METLA account comes back on a new device through the recovery key, if it got into the cloud backup, or through a linked Google account (section 3).

6. Retention and account deletion

How long we keep it

  • The account, the public part of the recovery key, the family and its payment history — until you delete the account.
  • The record of an entered tester email — up to a day; the wrong-attempt counter — up to two days; the email on the tester list — while testing goes on or until you ask us to remove it.
  • The IP address fingerprint — about a day.
  • Google Cloud server logs — up to 30 days. They may contain the account and family IDs, the IP address, a purchase token fingerprint, the plan’s product ID, a support request number, and — if sending the code email fails — a masked tester email (first letter and domain).
  • Backups of our database: daily backups — up to 30 days; point-in-time recovery — up to 7 days. Data of a deleted account disappears from them when these periods end.
  • Firebase sign-in service: IP addresses — a few weeks; linked Google account data after deletion — in backups for up to 180 days.
  • Analytics — 2 months (aggregated reports without device IDs — longer); crash reports — 90 days.
  • Emails to support — as long as needed to reply; closed requests in the bot database — 12 months after closing; your bot language record and the copies in our mailbox and internal chat and channel — until you ask us to delete them; at your request we delete them sooner — within 30 days. Bot hosting logs — per Railway’s retention periods.
  • The “account deleted, when” marker — indefinitely (see below).

How to delete your account

In the app: Profile → Account & data → Delete account. Or write to us (section 1) — we will delete it within 5 business days; the steps are on the account deletion page.

Erased on the server: the family (if it has no members — together with its payment history and invite code) or your family membership, nicknames, purchase links, tester confirmation (the account ID is removed from the list of confirmed accounts together with the last-check entry) and records of emails entered from this account, the public part of the recovery key, sign-in, the “fingerprint → account” record (for a Family owner).

Erased on the device: the recovery key (also from the cloud copy), the nickname (back to “METLA User”), “About me” and the profile photo, the saved tester email, family information, analytics data and the installation ID. After deletion METLA creates a new anonymous account by itself; a Family purchase is not attached to it automatically — only if you tap “Restore purchases” or buy again. If we delete the account at your request by email, nothing changes on the device: this data stays there until you uninstall METLA; the recovery key may remain, but it no longer works.

What remains: a deletion marker — under the random ID of the deleted account, only “deleted” and “when”, with no name, email, device or purchase data; it is kept indefinitely so that a copy of the app still signed in with the deleted account cannot restore it, rejoin a family or claim a purchase. Google Cloud logs — up to 30 days. Backups of our database — up to 30 days (point-in-time recovery — up to 7 days). Tester code emails — until you ask us to delete them; support requests — per the periods in section 3. Analytics and crash reports already sent are not linked to the account and are kept for their own periods. On the device, the cleaning history, Quarantine and the Radar list remain.

A family owner whose family still has members first removes them in Family (they immediately lose Pro from that family) or writes to us, and then deletes the account. Deleting the account does not cancel a Google Play subscription: cancel it in Google Play. Uninstalling METLA does not delete the account, and the server does not remove abandoned accounts by itself — so if you want the data erased, delete the account first and then uninstall METLA, or write to us.

10. Security

Everything that leaves the device is sent over a secure connection (HTTPS). The app never accesses our database directly — only through our server functions, and requests pass the app integrity check — except two: entering an activation code and deleting an account (deletion only erases the caller’s own account and is rate-limited per hour). On the device, METLA’s data is protected by your screen lock and Android encryption; on Android 11 and above Quarantine is in shared storage (section 5).
